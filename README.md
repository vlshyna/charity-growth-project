# 🐴 Charity Growth Project

> An end-to-end growth and digital development project for a charity foundation dedicated to rescuing and caring for horses.

The project combines data analytics, social media strategy, marketing, experimentation, web development, and AI-powered tools to explore how a small charity can strengthen its digital presence, reach more people, and ultimately increase its ability to receive support.

---

## 🎯 Project Goal

The overall goal is to help the foundation build a stronger and more sustainable digital presence and explore ways to increase awareness, engagement, and donations.

> The project follows a data-driven approach:
> Data → Insights → Decisions → Action → Measurement → Iteration

Rather than implementing changes based only on assumptions, the project aims to:

- understand the foundation's current situation;
- collect and analyze relevant data;
- identify the main problems and opportunities;
- formulate and test hypotheses;
- implement solutions;
- measure their impact;
- iterate based on the results.

---

## 🐎 About the Foundation

The foundation rescues horses and provides them with ongoing care, food, and rehabilitation.

Its work is largely supported by voluntary contributions and donations. Caring for rescued horses requires significant and continuous financial resources, including expenses such as food, veterinary care, transportation, and everyday maintenance.

One of the current challenges is therefore not only the direct cost of caring for the animals, but also reaching enough people who are willing and able to support the foundation.

---

## 🔎 Current Situation

The foundation currently has a presence on social media, primarily through Instagram (@karpovich_liza__, UA:Засновник фонду Фрі Хорсес, ENG:Founder of the Free Horses Foundation).

However, most of the available information about the foundation is concentrated within the social media page itself.

This creates several potential problems:

- information about the foundation is scattered across individual posts;
- it can be difficult for a new visitor to understand who the foundation is and what it does;
- information about the horses, projects, activities, and ways to help may not be easily accessible;
- social media content is not necessarily the best format for presenting structured and comprehensive information;
- the foundation has limited visibility beyond its existing social media audience;
- there is currently limited publicly accessible information that establishes a clear digital presence for the organization.

The foundation also faces ongoing financial challenges associated with caring for rescued horses.

One immediate example is the need to raise funds for hay and other essential resources for the horses.

---

## 💡 Initial Hypothesis

A stronger and more structured digital presence may help the foundation:

1. communicate its mission more clearly;
2. build trust with people discovering it for the first time;
3. make information easier to access;
4. provide clear and structured ways to support the foundation;
5. reach audiences beyond its existing social media followers;
6. create a stronger foundation for future marketing and fundraising activities.

### Initial hypothesis

> A dedicated website, combined with a structured social media and communication strategy, may increase awareness, trust, engagement, and ultimately the foundation's ability to attract support.

This hypothesis will be refined and evaluated using research and data rather than treated as an established fact.

# 🌐 Phase 1 — Website

The first stage of the project is the creation of a dedicated website for the foundation.

The website is intended to provide a centralized and structured source of information about:

- the foundation;
- its mission and values;
- rescued horses;
- current activities and projects;
- completed and ongoing fundraising campaigns;
- financial needs;
- ways to help;
- volunteering opportunities;
- contact information;
- social media channels.

### 💰 Fundraising Progress
An important component of the website will be the ability to communicate the current state of fundraising campaigns.

For example:

> Hay Fundraising Campaign  
> ₴XXX,XXX / ₴XXX,XXX  
> ███████████░░░░░ XX%

The goal is to make the current situation immediately understandable to a visitor and reduce the amount of effort required to understand what the foundation currently needs and how a person can help.

The technical implementation may use a lightweight data source such as a spreadsheet or another simple backend so that fundraising information can be updated without modifying the website's source code.

# 📊 Phase 2 — Data Collection & Social Media Analytics

After establishing the initial digital presence, the next stage will focus on collecting and analyzing data from the foundation's existing social media presence.

Access to Instagram analytics will be requested from the foundation.

Potential data points include:

### Audience

- follower count;
- follower growth;
- audience demographics;
- geographic distribution;
- active audience periods.

### Content

- post type;
- topic;
- format;
- publication time;
- frequency;
- calls to action;
- educational vs. emotional vs. fundraising content.

### Performance

- reach;
- impressions;
- likes;
- comments;
- shares;
- saves;
- profile visits;
- link clicks;
- engagement rate;
- follower conversion.

### Fundraising

Where data is available:

- campaign reach;
- campaign duration;
- donation volume;
- donation sources;
- traffic to fundraising pages;
- conversion from social media to donation.

The purpose of collecting these metrics is not simply to determine which posts perform well, but to investigate why they perform well and how this information can inform future decisions.

---

# 🧠 Phase 3 — Research & Problem Definition

Before implementing larger marketing changes, the project will investigate the foundation's current position and its potential audiences.

Potential research areas include:

- audience needs and motivations;
- donor behavior;
- trust factors in charitable organizations;
- communication patterns used by successful animal charities;
- competitor and benchmark analysis;
- fundraising communication;
- social media behavior;
- website usability;
- digital trust and transparency.

Where appropriate, external research and academic literature will be used to support assumptions and decisions.

The goal is to distinguish between:

> What I think might work

and

> What the available evidence suggests might work.

---

# 📈 Phase 4 — Marketing & Content Strategy

Based on the research and collected data, a marketing strategy will be developed.

Potential areas include:

- audience segmentation;
- positioning;
- content pillars;
- storytelling;
- fundraising communication;
- educational content;
- community-building;
- calls to action;
- social media growth;
- donor journeys;
- retention and recurring support.

The strategy will focus not only on asking people for donations, but also on answering:

> Why should someone care about this foundation?

and

> Why would they want to follow, support, recommend, or become involved with it?

---

# 🧪 Phase 5 — Experimentation

Marketing decisions will be treated as hypotheses that can be tested.

Potential experiments may include:

- A/B testing different calls to action;
- testing different fundraising messages;
- comparing emotional vs. informational content;
- testing different content formats;
- testing different posting times;
- testing different landing page structures;
- testing different donation-page messaging;
- testing different storytelling approaches.

Each experiment will follow a basic structure:

Hypothesis → Experiment → Metric → Result → Conclusion → Next iteration

Failed experiments will also be documented.

A negative result is still useful if it provides evidence that helps improve the next decision.

- fundraising progress tracking;
- landing pages;
- social media content systems;
- analytics dashboards;
- automation;
- AI-assisted workflows;
- other tools identified during the project.

The technologies used will depend on the specific problem being solved rather than being selected for the sake of using a particular technology.
